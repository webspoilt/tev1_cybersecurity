# The Development Journey of Tev1: From a Small Experimental Language Model to a Cybersecurity Triage Fine-Tuning Research Project

## Abstract & Chronicle Scope

This document chronicles the complete research and engineering trajectory of fine-tuning **`togethercomputer/Tev1-0.8B-experimental`** for structured defensive cybersecurity decision-making. Operating under strict zero-budget constraints and relying entirely on a local consumer laptop (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU with 4.00 GB VRAM), the project navigated architectural bottlenecks, conducted exhaustive audits of open-source security corpora, executed 4-bit Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA) parameter-efficient fine-tuning, subjected the resulting adapter to deep adversarial generalization audits, and executed a zero-overlap grouped template-family holdout experiment.

Crucially, this journey documents both successes and critical empirical setbacks—including the rejection of large-scale candidate datasets, the identification of severe base-model blind spots, the resolution of runtime latency anomalies, the definitive discovery of verification-clause template leakage in synthetic triage data, and the empirical measurement of out-of-distribution template transfer.

---

## 1. Project Inception & Research Motivation

### The Autonomous Defensive Dilemma
Modern Security Operations Centers (SOCs) face an overwhelming deluge of telemetry alerts generated by Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) agents, Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS), identity providers, and cloud audit logs. While commercial large language models (LLMs) demonstrate reasoning capabilities, their deployment in sensitive security contexts is frequently precluded by data privacy boundaries, cloud latency jitter, and unconstrained conversational preambles.

### Why Tev1-0.8B?
To address these challenges, the project targeted **`togethercomputer/Tev1-0.8B-experimental`**, an open-weight, 545.92-million parameter linear-attention / recurrent delta-rule hybrid model based on the Qwen 0.8B backbone. Tev1’s sub-billion parameter scale presents a compelling hypothesis: can a compact, edge-capable model be fine-tuned via parameter-efficient methods on consumer-grade hardware to execute high-precision, low-latency cybersecurity incident triage?

## 2. Chronological Milestones: Phases 0 through 7

### Phase 0: Hardware Profiling & Environment Standardization
- **Platform:** Local NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU (4.00 GB Dedicated GDDR6 VRAM, WDDM 3.1, Driver 566.07).
- **Environment:** Standardized on Python 3.11.9, PyTorch `2.6.0+cu124`, `transformers==5.22.2`, `peft==0.14.0`, and `bitsandbytes==0.45.2`.

### Phase 1: Gate 1 Baseline Evaluation (MCQ Benchmarks)
- **Benchmark:** 1,121 held-out cybersecurity multiple-choice questions (`val_split_v1.jsonl`: 199 WMDP-Cyber + 922 CyberMetric-10000).
- **Baseline Results:** **61.73% Accuracy**, **62.12% Macro F1**, **0.98% Invalid Format Rate**, **234.7 ms Latency**, **1.22 GB VRAM**. Outperformed reference `Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base` (58.97% accuracy, 573.89 ms latency).

### Phase 2: Candidate Dataset Audits & Discoveries
- **Trendyol Cybersecurity (53,202 rows):** **REJECTED.** 100% long-form essays (~276 words/response) without tactical decision choices.
- **LANL Cyber1 (1.64B events):** **REJECTED.** Anonymized tabular authentication CSV logs without textual telemetry or tactical choices.
- **OpenSec-Triage (50,000 alerts):** **APPROVED.** Balanced 7-class triage ground truth with EDR alert context and verification findings under CC BY 4.0.

### Phase 3: LoRA Architecture & Initial 50-Step Pilot
- **Architecture:** 12 linear projection target modules across self-attention and linear-attention delta-rule layers ($r=8$, $\alpha=16$, 5.41M trainable parameters).
- **Pilot Execution:** 50 optimizer steps on 3,500 candidate training samples. Train loss decreased from 4.332 to **0.8798**; eval loss reached **0.8757**. Checkpoint saved to `v2_experiments/tev1_qlora_pilot_v1`.

### Phase 4: Controlled Evaluation & Empirical Comparison
- **Validation Run (700 Samples):** Accuracy: **97.00%**, Macro F1: **97.05%**, Invalid Rate: **0.00%**.
- **Base vs. Adapter Delta:** Pure base model achieved **35.57% accuracy** (0.0% recall on `expected_admin`, 2.0% recall on `misconfiguration`, and 51.4% bias toward Option B). The pilot adapter delivered a **+61.43 percentage-point gain**, recovering 436 base model errors while regressing on only 6.

### Phase 5: Deep Generalization Audits & Template Leakage
- **Template Leakage Discovery:** Pairwise structural comparison revealed that while exact duplicates were 0%, **100% of validation records shared identical trailing verification clauses** drawn from a closed pool of ~14–15 fixed templates per class.
- **Ablation Test:** Removing trailing verification clauses caused accuracy to collapse from **97.00%** to **14.71%**, with the model defaulting to Option B (`suspicious`) on 95.6% of alerts. Proved that raw telemetry was identical across conflicting classes for 149 records; the verification sentences carried 100% of the decisive ground truth.

### Phase 6: Grouped Template Split Construction
- Audited all 4,200 candidate records, identifying **exactly 5 core template families per class** (35 families total).
- Partitioned into a 4:1 holdout split: **3,423 train records** (28 families) and **777 val records** (7 held-out families) with **0.0% template overlap** (`tev1_grouped_template_manifest.json`).

### Phase 7: Grouped Template-Family Holdout Evaluation (Executed)
With explicit authorization, the 50-step grouped-template experiment was executed:
- **Runner:** `run_tev1_grouped_pilot.py` targeting `v2_experiments/tev1_grouped_template_pilot_v1`.
- **Training Runtime:** 735.8 seconds (~12.3 minutes). Train loss converged from 4.331 to **0.867**; peak VRAM reached 2.54 GB.
- **Evaluation on 777 Held-Out Samples:**
  - Accuracy: **93.69%** (728 / 777 correct).
  - Macro F1: **92.81%**.
  - Invalid Output Rate: **0.00%** (0 / 777).
  - Inference Latency: **537.7 ms / sample**; Peak VRAM: **874.30 MB**.
- **Error Taxonomy (49 errors / 6.31%):**
  - **Class E $\rightarrow$ Class D Confusion (38 errors):** The held-out phrasing for security testing (*"validation job <SEC_ID>"*) was conflated with operational administration changes.
  - **Class G $\rightarrow$ Class B Confusion (10 errors):** Telemetry attribution disagreements were conservatively flagged as suspicious.
  - **Class C $\rightarrow$ Class B Confusion (1 error):** A single benign event flagged as suspicious.
- **Generalization Verdict:** The minor drop from 97.00% to 93.69% under 0.0% template overlap proves that the adapter is **not merely memorizing fixed verbatim strings**—it transfers semantic understanding of attack telemetry, baseline deviations, and change records to unseen phrasing archetypes.

## 3. Final Strategic Recommendations & Future Roadmap

1. **Preserve Both Adapters:**
   - `tev1_qlora_pilot_v1`: Verified baseline correction on the original benchmark.
   - `tev1_grouped_template_pilot_v1`: Proved 93.69% out-of-distribution transfer under 0.0% template leakage.
2. **Transition to Naturalistic Corpora:** Direct future fine-tuning toward authentic, human-authored SOC datasets—specifically **Splunk Boss of the SOC (BOTS v1–v3)** and **CIC-IDS Incident Narratives**—where unstructured notes and natural packet captures provide authentic evaluation rigor.
3. **Future Bounty Hunter Integration:** Maintain the architectural boundary separating Tev1 from the separate Bounty Hunter project, preparing standalone adapter export for future edge integration testing under human-in-the-loop controls.

### Phase 8: Independent Enterprise SOC Alert Evaluation with SecAlertBench (Executed)
To establish whether the 93.69% grouped-template accuracy transferred to naturalistic enterprise security alerts, we evaluated both the unadapted base model and the grouped LoRA adapter on **SecAlertBench** (`Dxsssu/SecAlertBench`):
- **Balanced Protocol:** 200 real-world enterprise alerts (100 Attack, 100 Non-Attack) with 50/50 option-order counterbalancing.
- **Condition A (Full SIEM Alert Metadata):** The grouped adapter improved accuracy from 45.0% to **61.5%** (+16.5 pp) and cut the **False Positive Rate from 75.0% to 26.0%** (-49.0 pp).
- **Condition B (Raw Telemetry Evidence Only):** When direct rule names and taxonomy tags were stripped, attack detection collapsed to **4.0%** (2.0% for base model), proving that the model relies on signature descriptors rather than raw URL-encoded payload comprehension.
- **Strategic Verdict:** Proven semantic transfer for structured alert interpretation, but confirmation that Tev1 is not an autonomous triage replacement for human analysts.
